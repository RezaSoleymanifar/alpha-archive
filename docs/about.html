About

What this is, what gets in, and how a paper is judged.

What this is

Alpha Archive is a quantitative research replication platform. We take quantitative finance research and turn it into reproducible implementations.

A paper's claim is a number printed in a table. A replication is that number recomputed from data anyone can fetch, with the difference shown rather than described. Everything here is one or the other, and the page says which.

The three gates

Roughly four in five papers do not make it in, and that ratio is the product rather than a shortcoming. A paper is indexed only if all three hold.

gatewhat it askswhat fails it
mechanismdoes it name a signal, a portfolio rule or a forecast?governance, disclosure and policy essays
datacan a stranger fetch the inputs for nothing? tick data, option chains, analyst estimates, TRACE, 13F panels
positiondoes it end in something you could hold? convergence theorems, existence proofs, mean-field games

How a paper is judged

Reproducing a number answers whether we got the same figure. It says nothing about whether the figure would have survived the checks the paper did not run, and most of this literature predates them. So a replication is scored twice.

checkwhat it catchesfrom
Newey-West tdaily returns are autocorrelated, so an ordinary standard error is too small and the t-statistic built on it too largeNewey & West, 1987
combinatorial purged CVa result that lives in one stretch of the sample: hold that stretch out and it collapses Lopez de Prado, ch. 12
probability of overfittingwhether choosing this specification from the ones tried would have held up out of sample Bailey, Borwein, Lopez de Prado & Zhu, 2017
minimum backtest lengthwhether the sample is even long enough to settle the question at this trial count Bailey, Borwein, Lopez de Prado & Zhu, 2014
deflated Sharpethe bar a Sharpe has to clear once the number of attempts is countedBailey & Lopez de Prado, 2014

Every threshold is written down and frozen before a run. On the first replication a criterion was set at 0.6, the result came back at 0.74, and it was called validated. That is grading your own exam, and worse, 0.6 had been chosen after seeing that 0.9 was unreachable. The thresholds live in code now and nothing in a run can change one.

What a status means

badgemeans
replicatedcleared a criterion written before the run, against a fixture we did not produce
notebook builtthe exhibits are rebuilt and compared cell by cell, but not yet scored against the claim
differsthe numbers were recomputed and they do not match
unverifiedthe fixture could not be fetched, so nothing can be claimed either way
not attemptedindexed and open for anyone to run

There is no middle tier. "Partially validated" is the phrase you reach for when you want credit you have not earned.

Where the data comes from

Free sources only, fetched at run time rather than mirrored: SEC EDGAR, FRED and ALFRED, the Ken French library, Open Source Asset Pricing, FINRA, CFTC, CBOE, the ECB and the US Treasury, mostly through Vintage, which carries two dates on every row so a backtest cannot read a number before it was public. Where a replication reaches outside that, the notebook says so.

Contributing

Pick a paper from the index, run it, and open a pull request with the gap table. A replication that fails is as useful as one that holds, and considerably more useful than one that was never attempted.
